Voice Call Phishing

How Does Vishing Work?

Step-by-step breakdown of voice manipulation and credential theft over phone calls

Authority Impersonation: Scammers spoof telephone numbers to pose as bank security, regulators, or internal IT helpdesk staff.
Golden Defense Rule: Never reveal passwords, OTPs, or financial pins over an incoming phone call. Always hang up and verify directly.
Attack Lifecycle

What is Social Engineering?

How psychological manipulation leads from initial lure to credential and data theft

Multi-Stage Attack: Lure begins with an email/PDF attachment → malicious execution → background credential harvesting → data exfiltration.
Human Firewall Defense: Stop the attack chain at Step 2 by verifying unexpected attachments before opening them.
Real Attack Example

Malicious APK Delivery (Trojan Malware)

Threat actors masquerade as couriers (e.g., J&T Express) sending malicious Android APKs to infect phones and steal OTPs

Malicious Installation (.apk): Legitimate couriers never distribute tracking apps via WhatsApp file attachments. Installing an APK bypasses app store vetting to intercept SMS banking OTPs and credentials.
Golden Rule: Never download or install unknown APKs from chat messages. Immediately block the sender and report to IT Security.

Human Error & Operational Risk

The Weakest Link: The Human Factor

Accidental commands, misplaced semicolons, and overlooked permissions often bypass every technical firewall.

Accidental Execution: Running DELETE FROM USERS; due to an unintended semicolon executes against the entire table before the WHERE filter is evaluated.
Human vs. Machine Defense: Firewalls protect networks, but internal operational guardrails and peer reviews are essential to prevent catastrophic human mistakes.

Good Example of a Human Firewall

How to Spot a Phishing Attempt

Urgency & Fear

"Your account will be suspended in 24 hours!" — Attackers create artificial panic to force quick, uncalculated action.

Unexpected Attachments / Links

Never click on links or open unexpected attachments (e.g. .zip, .exe, macros) if you weren't expecting them.

Generic Greetings

Uses generic salutations like "Dear Customer" or "Undisclosed Recipients" instead of addressing you specifically by name.

Mismatched Sender Address

Looks like official email, but inspect closely:
mis@revenuemonster.my vs mis@revenuernonster.my

Pay special attention to typosquatting domains (like 'rn' replacing 'm'). Always check the actual sender email headers.

Verify & Protect

Locking the Front Door

Principle 1

Length over Complexity

Use memorable passphrases with 16+ characters (e.g. 'Il0veRM!@' or multi-word phrases) rather than short scrambled words.

Principle 2

No Re-use

Never reuse your Revenue Monster corporate password for personal social media, streaming, or external sites.

Principle 3

Multi-Factor Auth (MFA)

The critical second layer of defense. Approve login prompts ONLY if you personally initiated them right now.

MFA fatigue attacks occur when attackers spam prompts. Emphasize: NEVER click approve on an unexpected prompt.
